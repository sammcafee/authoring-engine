# The Class Nobody Signed Up For

I offered a beginner woodworking class at the community shop last spring.
Six weeks, four students. Two dropped after the first session, which stung
more than it should have, because I'd built the whole curriculum around a
tidy six-week arc and now I had two people and a hole in the middle of my
plan.

I could have run it anyway, thinner. Instead I scrapped the arc and asked
the two who stayed what they actually wanted to build. One wanted a bookshelf
for a kid's room. The other wanted to fix a wobbly chair she'd inherited and
didn't know how to talk about with a straight face at a family dinner where
everyone had opinions about it.

Neither project fit a curriculum. Both were better classes than the one I'd
planned, because the thing driving the work wasn't "learn joinery," it was
"stop being embarrassed by this specific chair." Skills follow motivation,
not the other way around, and I'd built the whole thing backwards on the
assumption that people show up wanting the skill first.

I still have the six-week plan saved somewhere. I haven't used it since.
