# The Broken Jig

I snapped the fence on my table saw jig on a Tuesday, the kind of Tuesday
where nothing else was going wrong, which is exactly when you make the cut
you shouldn't. Twelve years I'd used that jig. Never once measured it against
a straightedge to check it was still square.

It wasn't square. It hadn't been square for at least a year, probably two.
Every panel I'd cut in that stretch was off by a hair, and I'd been sanding
the difference away without asking why the difference was there.

The easy story is "check your tools more often." True, and useless, because
everyone already knows it and nobody does it consistently, including me,
including now. The actual lesson was smaller and more annoying: I trusted a
tool because it was familiar, not because I'd verified it. Familiarity and
accuracy are not the same thing, and a jig that's been reliable for a decade
can still drift out from under you without a single dramatic failure to
announce it.

I built a new fence that weekend. Squared it three times. It'll drift too,
eventually. The difference is I wrote the date on the underside, so next time
I'll at least know how long I trusted it before I checked.
